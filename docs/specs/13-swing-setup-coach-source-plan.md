Swing Set-Up Coach: plan to turn “What Your Kettlebell Swing Is Missing” into an AI tool
Nov 20, 2026 · @Someone · Plan only, nothing built yet
Summary
We turn the program into Swing Set-Up Coach: the member films their set-up from the side, the app measures it against Mike's 7 steps, and an AI coach explains what to fix, using Mike's own words and the exact moment in his video that shows the fix.
The set-up is a good fit for this because it is a held position, not a fast movement. A held position can be measured reliably from a phone camera; a full swing at speed mostly cannot. Mike's main claim in the ebook is that the biggest swing mistake happens before the bell moves, so we only measure where the measurement is trustworthy.
Three things make it a real tool rather than videos with a checklist:
1.  Measured, not self-reported. Body landmarks give numbers: shin angle, whether the hips sit between knee and shoulder height, the head–mid-back–tailbone line, shoulder-to-ear gap, and toe-to-bell distance using the bell as a ruler.
2.  A coach that only says what Mike said. Every correction is tied to a quoted cue and a timestamped clip from his videos, and nothing outside the source material.
3.  Consistency over time. Mike's standard is that the first rep should look like the last. The tool compares the set-up across sets and sessions, and checks the bottom of the backswing against the set-up (his Step 2 point), which no checklist can do.
Around the check sit four supporting modes: a hands-free Approach ritual, a guided Feet activation drill, the ebook's 2-week EMOM swing program with a timer and logging, and a coach chat limited to set-up and swing topics.

The ebook adds three things that are not in these videos: The Approach (stand 2–3 ft behind the bell, 3 belly breaths, picture the rep, step right then left, run the checklist), the set-up checklist, and the 2-week swing program (6 sessions on the minute, 8 to 14 reps, 3–8 sets, starting bell about 16 kg for men and 8 kg for women, plus goblet squats, reverse hypers and side plank raises). Those three bonus lessons have their own videos on Vimeo (ids 346097462, 346096258, 346162445), but the files were not part of this upload.
Contradictions to settle with Mike before we score anything:
• Bell distance: the ebook says "5 – 1.5 feet", but its checklist and the video both say 0.5–1.5 ft. Use 0.5–1.5 ft.
• Feet: the ebook says to grip the floor and "cork-screw" the feet; the video says toes stay flat and relaxed, with a 10–20% press through the tips. The coach needs one version.
• Effort level: the video gives 10–15% for the first foot, 15–20% for the second and 20% at the bell. Treat it as a range, not a single number.
• Load and stance: heavier bells mean standing closer to the bell (the video's 18 kg versus double 40 kg example). The check should use the member's bell weight when judging distance.
The tool: one check and the modes around it
The Set-Up Check is the core; the other four modes either prepare the member for it or put its result to use.
1. Profile (first visit only, under a minute)
Six questions that change the coaching, each taken from something Mike says changes the advice:
• Neck history: yes means head guidance stays neutral, never "tip it up" (Step 5).
• Hamstrings: tight, normal or hypermobile. Tight gets the evening flexibility and contract-relax advice; hypermobile gets "you won't feel the stretch, use the shin angle instead" (Step 2).
• Bell: type (competition steel or cast iron), weight, single or double. These set the expected distance from the toes and the grip advice (Steps 1 and 6).
• Hand size: small, average or large. Decides which fingers go inside the window (Step 6).
• Footwear: barefoot, minimalist or trainers. The ebook recommends barefoot or minimalist.
• Goal: learn the set-up, fix a known fault, or start the 2-week program.
2. Set-Up Check (camera)
1.  Placement coach. Phone on the floor or a chair, side-on, 2–3 m away, at hip height. A live outline shows when the whole body and the bell are in frame and the camera really is side-on (checked from how far apart the two shoulders appear).
2.  The approach, spoken. Voice prompts run Mike's approach: stand 2–3 ft behind the bell, take 3 belly breaths, step up right foot then left. The phone needs no touching.
3.  Hold the set-up for 3 seconds. The app takes the steadiest frames. Optional: turn and repeat facing the camera, which adds stance width and shoulder-to-ear gap.
4.  Optional: do 3 swings. This adds the tail check (Step 6), the tip-back before the hike (Step 7), and the bottom of the backswing compared with the set-up (Step 2).
5.  Scorecard. All 7 steps in Mike's order, each marked good, adjust, or "can't see it" when the camera could not judge. Each one shows the member's frame next to Mike's reference frame, the measured number, and a 5–20 second clip of Mike fixing exactly that.
6.  One fix only. The coach picks the single fault that matters most (the order of priority is in the AI section) and sets a re-check. Mike teaches one cue at a time, and so does the tool.
3. Approach ritual (no camera)
A hands-free audio routine for every set, built from the ebook's Approach bonus and checklist. It also runs inside the program timer, so the ritual happens before each set rather than once.
4. Feet activation drill
The staggered-stance tripod drill from Step 1: lift and spread the toes, set them down, press 10–20% through the tips for 5 seconds, relax, 3–5 times per foot, then both feet together, with the breath (exhale on the press, inhale on the release). A timer and Mike's own voice, cut from the Step 1 video, guide it; the gecko cue appears at the right moment.
5. 2-week swing program
The ebook's 6 sessions as an on-the-minute timer, with the approach ritual before each set and a log of reps, sets and how each set felt.
• Starting bell: suggested from the profile (about 16 kg for men, 8 kg for women, per the ebook) or taken from the existing Kettlebell Selector result.
• Rep counting (later phase): counted on the phone from hip extension. If 14 reps take 30 seconds, the remaining 30 is the rest, exactly as the ebook explains.
• At the end: the coach suggests how to run it again, changing load and sets as the ebook advises, using the logged sets.
• Extras: goblet squats, reverse hypers and side plank raises, 2–4 sets of 8–12, when the program is used on its own.
6. Coach chat
The member asks about their own result ("why does my shin angle matter?"). The coach answers from the source material, refers to their measured numbers, and can open any clip. Anything outside set-up and swing topics, including pain, gets a polite redirect.
Measurement spec
The numbers come from code, not from the AI. A pose model finds 33 body points in each frame on the phone; plain geometry turns them into the measures below; the AI only explains the results. Every pass range is a draft for Mike to sign off, and will be tuned on his own correct and incorrect demo frames (see Evaluation).
Before the set-up, the member stands tall for 2 seconds. That frame calibrates the measures to their body: their torso length becomes the unit of measure, and their relaxed shoulder-to-ear gap becomes the baseline.

Rules that keep it honest:
• Below the confidence bar, say so. Any check the camera cannot see clearly (body cut off, front-on when side-on is needed, occluded) shows "can't see it" plus how to film it, never a guess.
• Never shows the raw 33 points. The member sees a simple overlay of three or four lines that match Mike's own teaching: the stick line, the shin line, the hip band and the head line.
AI agent design
The agent is a coach that reasons over measured facts and Mike's words; it never measures and never invents. It has three jobs: explain an assessment, answer follow-up questions, and adjust the 2-week program from the logged sets.
What it always has in context. All the source material fits in one prompt: the 8 transcripts are about 5,800 words and the ebook about 2,900, roughly 12,000 tokens. So there is no search index to build or keep in sync; the whole course is cached in the prompt, and the coach always has every cue in front of it. Each transcript line carries its video time once the videos are transcribed.

One fix, chosen by safety first. When several things are off, the coach fixes one, in this order. The order follows Mike's own reasons: protect the spine, then bending force, then loading the hips, then the finer points.

A fixed answer shape, checked before it is shown. The coach returns structured fields: main fault, why it matters, Mike's cue, clip, drill, when to re-check, confidence, and up to two other notes. The server checks three things:
• Every "Mike's cue" must appear word for word in the source. A string check against the transcripts and ebook throws out anything paraphrased or invented.
• Every clip id must exist in the clip index.
• Any number the coach mentions must match a number in the assessment.
If a check fails, the coach is asked once more; if it fails again, the member sees the plain scorecard with no AI text.
Where the vision model is used. Only for what geometry cannot do:
• Is the bell visible, and where is it?
• Does the back outline look reliable, or is clothing in the way?
• The grip photo.
• Is the camera really side-on?
It can lower the confidence of a medium-confidence measure, never override a high-confidence one.
Voice. The coach speaks in Mike's style and quotes Mike, but is labelled "AI coach, trained on Mike's set-up course". It does not pretend to be Mike. Mike decides whether he wants that line held differently.
Guardrails.
• Pain, dizziness or shortness of breath: stop, see a professional (from the ebook disclaimer). No diagnosis, ever.
• Neck history: never suggest lifting the head.
• Off-topic questions: a short redirect to Mike's programs.
• Limits: a daily cap per member on checks and chat messages, a prompt version on every answer, and a log of every call for review.
Models. A vision-capable model for assessments and a smaller, faster one for chat, with the course text cached. The app currently calls AI through Lovable's gateway (LOVABLE_API_KEY), which is not set on Vercel; this needs a direct provider key before any AI work starts.
How the videos are used
The videos do five jobs: show the fix, supply Mike's reference poses, provide a ready-made test set, give the guided modes Mike's voice, and give every quote a timestamp.
1.  Timed transcripts. Transcribe the 8 MP4s with word-level timing. Every cue the coach quotes then links to the second Mike says it, and the transcripts already supplied become the check against which quotes are verified.
2.  Clip index (about 25–35 clips). A curated table: step, fault, video, start, end, Mike's line. Examples:
    ◦ Back rounding → the stick demo with Dr. Drew.
    ◦ Tail → the grip video, around 3:12.
    ◦ Tight hamstrings and tilted shins → the hips video.
    Clips play in the app's existing Vimeo player at the right second. For the scorecard, short silent loops (5–20 s, "incorrect, then correct") are cut from the original MP4s and stored with the app's media.
3.  Mike's reference poses. Run the same pose model on Mike's correct set-ups (side-on frames in the Back, Head/Neck and Hips videos). The results become the "Mike" frame beside the member's on the scorecard and the centre of the draft pass ranges.
4.  A free, labelled test set. In the Back, Head/Neck and Shoulders videos, Dr. Drew deliberately shows each fault: rounded upper back and over-arched low back (Back), head dropped and "broken neck" (Head/Neck), and a rounded back (Shoulders). Each frame is a fault already named by Mike, so the scoring code can be tested on it before any member films anything.
5.  Mike's voice in the guided modes. Audio cut from Step 1 (the 5-4-3-2-1 toe press, "tips of the toes"), Step 7 (inhale, navel in, hold) and the intro gives the Feet drill and Approach ritual his voice instead of a synthetic one.

Evaluation, safety and privacy
Nothing ships to members until the scoring agrees with Mike on at least 85% of labelled set-ups for every camera-checked step.
Test set, in three layers:
1.  Mike's videos: his correct set-ups and the faults Dr. Drew demonstrates, with frames taken across each demo (no filming needed).
2.  40–60 new side-on clips of 6–10 people of different heights and builds, in both cast-iron and competition bells, each set-up labelled by Mike step by step.
3.  Hard cases: baggy clothes, a dark room, the phone slightly off side-on, a child's height. These must come back as "can't see it", never as a wrong verdict.
Pass bar:
• At least 85% agreement with Mike on each camera-checked step.
• Wrong-direction advice (for example "stand closer" when they should stand further away) on under 2% of checks.
• 100% of quoted cues found word for word in the source.
• The AI's choice of the one fix matches Mike's on at least 75% of checks.
The test set re-runs every time a prompt or a pass range changes.
Safety:
• The ebook's disclaimer is shown before the first check, and the member accepts it.
• Pain, dizziness or shortness of breath: stop, see a professional, and the session ends.
• Neck history: head advice stays neutral.
• The tool comments only on the set-up, the first three swings and the program. It never judges load beyond the ebook's starting suggestions.
Privacy:
• Pose detection runs on the phone, so the camera feed is never sent.
• Keyframes are uploaded only with consent, private to the member, and deleted after 30 days unless kept.
• Members under 18 get the drills and clips but no camera check (to be confirmed with Mike).
• A "delete all my set-up data" button on the account page.
Phases and order of work
Build in five phases, each shippable on its own, so members get value before any AI is involved and the AI only arrives once its measures have been proven against Mike.
Phase 2 (highlighted) is the core of the product. Phase 0 is the step most likely to be skipped and the one that decides quality: without Mike's sign-off on the cues and pass ranges, every later phase is guessing.

Risks:
• Phone pose models are weaker side-on and with baggy clothing. That is why the confidence bar and "can't see it" exist, and why the back-outline check is rated medium.
• Tracking the bell is the hardest part. Kettlebells are not in standard object models. Toe-to-bell distance and the tail check need a small custom detector or the vision model. If neither is good enough by the Phase 2 gate, those two checks ship as photo prompts instead.
• Shin-angle and hip ranges vary by body shape. Long femurs or limited ankle range can fail a correct set-up. The test set needs those builds, and Mike may want ranges that vary by height.
• Cost. Each AI assessment is one cached course prompt plus 2–4 images. The daily cap and the cache keep the cost per member predictable; measure it in Phase 3 before opening the tool to everyone.
• Content gaps. Three bonus videos are missing as files, and the timestamps in this plan are drafts until the videos are transcribed.
