Kettlebell Roadmap Coach: plan to turn “Your Roadmap to Kettlebell Progression” into an AI tool
Nov 21, 2026 · @Someone
Summary
We turn the Roadmap into Kettlebell Roadmap Coach: the tool places the member in one of Mike's 4 phases by testing his own benchmarks, unlocks movements one by one as the member passes them, and builds follow-along sessions in the same format as Mike's workout videos. An AI coach decides when the member is ready to move up a phase.
Today the Roadmap asks members to judge their own level ("scan through and see which phase suits you best"). That is exactly where most people get it wrong. The PDFs already contain everything a placement test needs: concrete benchmarks with reps, sets and rest (20 spinal roll-ups, 5 × 10 swings with 60 s rest, 3–5 loaded half getups each side, and so on). The tool turns those numbers into tests the phone can count and time.
Four things make it more than a page of PDFs and clips:
1.  Placement by testing, not by guessing. A 15–20 minute benchmark session, counted and timed by the camera, places the member in a phase, and records which movements within it are passed or still being learned.
2.  A skill tree, not a list. Each phase's movements are separate items with their own state: learning, practising, benchmark passed. The next phase opens movement by movement, not all at once, and the PDF's own fallbacks are built in (Halo when overhead mobility is not there yet; staggered stance when the Row With Reciprocity is not).
3.  Mike's rep clips as the reference. The 9 clips each show one clean rep, most from the front and then from the side. The member's rep is lined up against Mike's, frame by frame, from the same angle.
4.  Sessions built the way Mike builds them. The "Train According To Your Phase" page says every workout needs a warm-up, breath training and decompression stretches. The full workout video shows the format: two tracks side by side (Level I and Level II), timed work intervals and a total-time clock. Generated sessions follow that shape, using the rep clips as looping demos.

Gaps and errors to settle before building:
• "Quality standards" are never written down. Phase 1 and 2 benchmarks depend on them. Phase 1 even shows a leftover editing note: "*reuse the material from KBL + add in quantity". Without Mike's standards per movement, nothing can be scored.
• The lunge clip is mislabelled. The course lists it under Phase 2 as "Reverse Lunge in Rack Position", but the clip shows an overhead reverse lunge, which is a Phase 3 movement. Phase 2 has no rack lunge clip.
• Eleven movements have no clip: Deadlift, Halo and Single Arm Row in Phase 1; Standing Overhead Press, Single Arm Farmer's Carry and Reverse Lunge in Rack Position in Phase 2; all five Phase 4 movements.
• One Phase 3 benchmark names no movement: "4 sets of 8 reps each arm with 30 sec. rest" (most likely the Single Arm Swing, given the sets and rest; needs confirming).
• "Moderate weight (for you)" is never defined. The tool needs a rule, for example a share of the bell picked in the Kettlebell Selector.
• The clips are only 240p. The Vimeo originals (ids already in the course) are needed for good reference poses.
The tool: test, map, practise, re-test
The tool runs a loop: test where the member really is, show it on a map, give sessions that work on the gaps, then re-test.
1. Placement test ("Find your phase", 15–20 min)
1.  Three questions: how long they have trained with kettlebells, any injuries, and which phase they think they are in.
2.  Overhead check (10 s, camera side-on). Arms overhead; the camera measures how far the arms reach. This decides Half Kneeling Overhead Press or Halo, the fallback the Phase 1 PDF already names.
3.  Adaptive benchmark run. Testing starts one phase below the member's own guess, not at Phase 1, so an experienced lifter is not made to do 30 bodyweight squats. Each benchmark passed moves the test up; the first clear fail stops it. The phone counts reps, times rest, and checks quality on the movements that have clips.
4.  Result: the phase, plus each movement's state within it. For example: "Phase 2. Swing and Overhead Press passed; Farmer's Carry posture and the rack lunge still to come."
2. The Roadmap map
All 4 phases on one screen, each movement a node with one of four states: locked, learning, practising, benchmark passed. Each node opens:
• Mike's clip, with the front and side views as separate loops.
• The member's last rep check next to Mike's rep.
• What to practise this week.
• Deeper instruction: the Mastering the Kettlebell lessons for that lift, picked from the app's existing lesson tags, which already cover dozens of deadlift, swing, clean, get-up, lunge and row lessons.
A phase's nodes open one by one, so a member can be in Phase 3 for the clean while still practising the Phase 2 farmer's carry.
3. Rep check
The member films one rep from the same angle as Mike's clip. The app lines it up against Mike's rep frame by frame and shows both side by side, with Mike as a faint "ghost" over the member. It reports on the standards for that movement (see Movement checks) and shows the one thing to change.
4. Phase sessions (follow-along)
The AI builds each session in the shape "Train According To Your Phase" asks for, and plays it in the format of the full workout video:
• Warm-up that raises core temperature and moves every joint.
• Breath training.
• 2–4 movement blocks from the member's phase, on two tracks like the video: a Level I (easier) and a Level II (harder) version of each, picked per movement from the map. A "learning" movement gets the easier track, a "practising" one the harder.
• Decompression stretches, especially for the spine.
Intervals, rest blocks and a total-time clock work as in the video. Mike's rep clips loop silently as the demo during each interval, and the warm-up and stretch videos come from KBL, which is already in the app.
5. Benchmark re-tests
When the coach thinks a movement is ready, it schedules a re-test of just that benchmark, run by the tool. For example, 5 × 10 swings with a 60-second rest timer and the reps counted, or 3–5 loaded half getups each side, with each getup scored stage by stage.
6. Nasal breathing check
The PDFs make nasal breathing a benchmark in Phases 1 and 3. During a session or benchmark, the front camera checks whether the lips stay closed, and reports the share of work time spent with the mouth shut. It is a proxy, labelled as such, and only runs with consent.
7. Coach chat and hand-offs
The member can ask the coach "why am I still in Phase 2?" or "what should I work on this week?". The coach also hands members on:
• Into KBL: Phase 1–2 to KBL Level I, Phase 3 to KBL Level II.
• To the Swing Set-Up Coach: before the 2-Hand Swing node opens.
• Into Mastering the Kettlebell: for any movement that stays stuck.
Movement checks
One engine checks every lift: the phone tracks body points in each frame and turns them into joint angles over time. That time series is then lined up with Mike's rep from the same angle, so a slow rep and a fast rep can still be compared phase by phase (start, lowest point, finish). The checks per lift are drafts taken from what the clips show. The PDFs never write the quality standards down, so Mike has to confirm or replace every line.

For each movement, the result is one of three outcomes:
• Meets standard: every checkpoint is inside its range.
• Close: one checkpoint is out. The single fix is shown, with Mike's clip from the same angle.
• Can't judge: the wrong angle, the body is cut off, or low confidence. The member is told how to film it again.
Movements with no clip (Deadlift, Halo, Single Arm Row, Standing Overhead Press, rack lunge and all of Phase 4) are checked against the same draft rules but without a ghost overlay, until Mike films reference reps. Clips matter most where the risk is highest, so the first new ones to film are the Deadlift and the Single Arm Row.
AI agent design
The agent plans and explains; fixed rules decide. It proposes sessions, phase moves and loads, but code checks every proposal against the member's data and the Roadmap's rules before the member sees it.
Four decisions it makes:
1.  Phase moves. A movement counts as passed when three things are true: the benchmark is met, the rep check meets standard on two separate days, and (in Phases 1 and 3) the breathing check is passed. A phase is passed when all its movements are. The agent proposes "move up" or "stay"; the rule has the final say. It can also propose a step back, for example when a Phase 3 member's clean keeps landing on "close".
2.  This week's sessions. It picks movements, the track (Level I or Level II), sets, reps, work and rest from an allowed list. The list holds only unlocked movements, the member's bells, their available time, and warm-up and stretch videos from KBL and the Library.
3.  Today's dose. If the member did KBL's readiness assessment today, the result carries over: a Recovery Day result turns the session into breath work and decompression stretches only.
4.  What "moderate weight (for you)" means. It starts from the Kettlebell Selector's suggestion and moves with logged effort and rep-check results. For example, if quality slips under a heavier bell, the next session goes back down.

Checks before anything is shown:
• A session may only use unlocked movements and the member's own bells.
• It must fit the time the member has, and must include a warm-up, breath training and decompression stretches, as "Train According To Your Phase" requires.
• Weekly volume per movement can rise by at most a set amount; the cap is for Mike to set.
• A phase move must match the pass rule.
If a check fails, the agent is asked to try once more. If it fails again, the member gets a rules-only session.
Grounding. The Roadmap PDFs hold under 1,000 words, too little for real coaching, and the clips are silent. So the coach draws on Mike's wider material that is already in the app:
• The Roadmap PDFs.
• The KBL lesson text.
• The Mastering the Kettlebell lessons for each movement.
To quote Mike, the MTK videos for these movements need transcripts (Phase 0). Until then, the coach links to the lesson rather than quoting it.
Guardrails:
• Pain, dizziness or shortness of breath stop the session.
• Injuries the member listed rule out the movements they affect, until the member clears them.
• No advice on loads beyond Phase 3 without benchmark data.
• Never pretends to be Mike.
• A daily cap per member and a prompt version on every answer.
The AI key is the same blocker as for the Swing Set-Up Coach: nothing is set on Vercel today.
How the videos are used
The single-rep clips do four jobs and the full workout does two; between them, every video is used for more than playback.
The 9 single-rep clips:
1.  Reference reps. Split each clip into its front view and side view, and run the pose model on each. The result is Mike's angle-over-time trace for each lift, from both sides. That trace is what the member's rep is lined up against, and what draws the ghost overlay.
2.  Checkpoint frames. The key moments in each rep (lowest point, rack, lockout) become still frames, shown next to the member's matching frame in the rep check.
3.  Interval demos. Each 6–12 second rep loops silently on screen while the member works, as the demo in phase sessions and benchmark re-tests.
4.  Map thumbnails. One frame per movement for the map nodes.
The full workout (Phase II Workout I):
1.  The session template. Its structure becomes the engine that plays generated sessions: teaching sets of 5–10 reps first, then timed work intervals (20 s on screen), rest blocks, a total-time countdown and a finisher. The same goes for its two-track layout, with the easier and harder version side by side under Level I and Level II labels.
2.  A ready-made pairing table. Its five pairs (for example Two Hand Deadlift with Two Hand Swing, and Lunge Row supported with unsupported) are Mike's own choices of easier and harder versions. They seed the Level I / Level II pairs the session builder picks from; Mike adds pairs for the other Roadmap movements.
The workout also stays playable as is, as the free "taste of KBL" follow-along the Train-by-Phase page offers. That page links Vimeo 412071887; check it is the same video.
Needed for this:
• HD originals of the 9 clips from Vimeo. The ids are already in the course (730402831 and the rest); 240p is too coarse for clean reference poses.
• The correct lunge clip. The Phase 2 rack lunge clip, or the course label fixed to say Overhead Reverse Lunge under Phase 3.
• New clips for the 11 movements without one, filmed the same way: one rep, front then side, about 10 seconds. That is a single short filming session.
• Timestamps for the full workout: when each exercise starts and ends. The on-screen labels make this a quick job, and it lets the session engine reuse individual segments.

Evaluation, safety and privacy
Placement is the highest-stakes decision, so it has the strictest bar: the tool must place at least 80% of test members in the same phase Mike would, and never more than one phase too high.
Test set:
• Mike's 9 clips: each must score "meets standard" against itself from the other camera angle. This is the sanity check that the engine works.
• 20–30 members across all four phases: each does the placement test and is also placed by Mike from video. This measures agreement on phase and on each node.
• Deliberately faulty reps: filmed by a coach (bell drifting away on the clean, torso twisting on the row, arm drifting in the getup). Each must come back "close" with the right fix, or "can't judge", never "meets standard".
• Rep counting: at least 98% exact on 50 benchmark sets (swings, squats, bridges). A benchmark is pass or fail on a count, so the count must be right.
Pass bar for the AI:
• 100% of proposed sessions pass the rule checks before they are shown.
• Mike rates at least 80% of a sample of 30 generated sessions as "I would give this".
• Phase-move proposals always match the pass rule.
Safety:
• The placement test is not run on a member who reports pain or a current injury in the movements it tests.
• Loaded benchmarks (swings, getups, cleans) come only after the bodyweight and light-bell ones in the same session.
• Placing too high is the dangerous error, so when in doubt the tool places lower.
• The Halo fallback is suggested on mobility, never forced.
Privacy: pose and face tracking run on the phone, the camera feed is never sent, and keyframes are uploaded only with consent and deleted after 30 days unless kept. The mouth-closed check uses face points only, with no face image stored. The same consent and "delete my data" controls are shared with the Swing Set-Up Coach.
Phases and order of work
Build after the Swing Set-Up Coach's camera phase, because this tool reuses its movement-check engine. Then ship in five phases, each one usable before the next starts.
Phase 1 alone already beats today's course: the map, benchmarks logged by hand and two-track sessions need no camera and no AI. Phase 0 is again the real constraint: without Mike's written quality standards, nothing beyond Phase 1 can be scored.

Risks:
• Placing someone too high is the one error that can injure. Hence: when in doubt the tool places lower, loaded tests come only after bodyweight tests, and there is no loaded test at all for anyone reporting pain.
• Quality checks are only as good as the standards. Until Mike writes them, the checks in this plan are informed drafts, not his method.
• The getup, carries and double-bell work are hard to check on camera. Expect more "can't judge" on these. The getup is scored by stage so that a partial result is still useful.
• The mouth-closed check measures lips, not airflow. It must always be shown as a guide, never as a fail on its own.
• The 240p clips limit the reference poses. Without the HD originals, ghost overlays will look rough and checkpoint frames will be soft.
• Scope creep into Phase 4. Snatches, double bells and rotational work need their own standards and clips; treat them as a later release.
